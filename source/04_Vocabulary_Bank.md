# Vocabulary Bank — P3 to P6

**How to learn vocabulary so it actually sticks:**
Do **not** memorise word lists in isolation. Use the 3-step method:

1. **Read** the word in its example sentence here.
2. **Write your own sentence** using it — about your own life, not a made-up one.
3. **Use it once** in your next composition. A word is only yours after you have used it.

Target: **10 words + 3 idioms per week.** That is ~500 words a year, which is more than enough.

## PART 6 — "Show, Don't Tell" Bank

Instead of naming an emotion, describe the body. This is the single fastest way to raise a composition's language mark.

| Instead of… | Write something like… |
|---|---|
| I was scared. | My throat tightened. My legs felt like jelly and refused to move. |
| I was angry. | My fists clenched at my sides and I felt heat rising up my neck. |
| I was sad. | A lump rose in my throat and the words would not come out. |
| I was nervous. | My palms grew clammy and I kept rubbing them on my shorts. |
| I was happy. | I could not stop the grin spreading across my face. |
| I was surprised. | My jaw dropped and I stood rooted to the spot. |
| I was embarrassed. | My cheeks burned and I stared hard at my shoes. |
| I was tired. | My eyelids felt weighted with lead. |
| I was relieved. | The knot in my stomach finally loosened. |
| I was guilty. | I could not meet her eyes. |
| It was hot. | My shirt clung to my back and sweat trickled down my temple. |
| It was noisy. | I could barely hear my own voice above the din. |

## PART 7 — The Five Senses Checklist

A strong descriptive paragraph uses at least **three** senses.

| Sense | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **Sight** | What colours, shapes, movements? |
| **Sound** | What can be heard in the background? |
| **Smell** | Is there an aroma, a stench, something faint? |
| **Touch** | Temperature, texture, weight, pain? |
| **Taste** | Only if relevant — food, blood, salt, dryness of the mouth |

> *The market was a riot of colour **(sight)**. Hawkers called out their prices over the clatter of woks **(sound)**, and the smoky aroma of grilled satay drifted through the humid air **(smell + touch)**.*

## PART 8 — Useful Words for Comprehension Answers

Comprehension open-ended answers score higher when you use precise inference verbs:

- The author **implies / suggests / indicates** that…
- This shows that the character was **reluctant / determined / remorseful**…
- The word "___" suggests that…
- This is because…
- As a result…
- In contrast…

**For "How do you know…?" questions:** always quote or refer to the evidence in the passage.
**For "Why…?" questions:** the answer must start in a way that answers *why* — usually "Because…" or "This is because…"
**For inference questions:** never lift a sentence wholesale; adapt the words to fit your answer.
