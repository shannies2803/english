## 1. Nouns

A noun names a person, place, thing, animal or idea.

### 1.1 Types

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Common | General name | boy, city, river |
| Proper | Specific name, always capitalised | Ravi, Singapore, Orchard Road |
| Collective | A group | a **flock** of birds, a **swarm** of bees |
| Abstract | Cannot be touched | honesty, fear, courage, happiness |
| Countable | Can be counted | one apple, two apples |
| Uncountable | Cannot be counted individually | water, rice, furniture, advice |
| Compound | Two words joined | toothbrush, mother-in-law, ice cream |

### 1.2 Common Collective Nouns (memorise these)

a **flock** of sheep/birds · a **herd** of cattle/elephants · a **school** of fish · a **swarm** of bees/locusts · a **pack** of wolves/cards · a **pride** of lions · a **litter** of puppies · a **colony** of ants · a **troop** of monkeys · a **bunch** of grapes/keys · a **bouquet** of flowers · a **fleet** of ships · a **gang** of thieves · a **crew** of sailors · a **team** of players · a **band** of musicians · a **cluster** of stars · a **suite** of rooms · a **galaxy** of stars · a **library** of books

### 1.3 Plurals

| Rule | Singular → Plural |
|---|---|
| Most nouns: add **-s** | book → books |
| Ends in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z: add **-es** | bus → buses, dish → dishes, watch → watches, box → boxes |
| Consonant + y: change y → **-ies** | baby → babies, city → cities |
| Vowel + y: just add **-s** | boy → boys, key → keys |
| Ends in -f/-fe: change to **-ves** | leaf → leaves, knife → knives, wolf → wolves, thief → thieves |
| Exceptions to -f rule | roof → roofs, chief → chiefs, belief → beliefs, cliff → cliffs |
| Consonant + o: add **-es** | potato → potatoes, tomato → tomatoes, hero → heroes |
| Vowel + o, or musical: add **-s** | radio → radios, piano → pianos, photo → photos |
| Irregular | man → men, woman → women, child → children, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, goose → geese, mouse → mice, ox → oxen, person → people |
| No change | sheep, deer, fish, aircraft, series, species, salmon |
| Only plural | scissors, trousers, spectacles, jeans, pyjamas, clothes, goods, belongings |

### 1.4 Uncountable Nouns (very commonly tested)

These are **always singular** and take a singular verb. They never take *a/an* and never add *-s*.

**information, advice, furniture, luggage, baggage, equipment, homework, housework, news, money, bread, butter, rice, water, sugar, traffic, weather, music, knowledge, progress, evidence, jewellery, machinery, stationery, scenery, litter, rubbish, poetry**

> ✅ The furniture **is** new. · ✅ She gave me some good **advice**.
> ⚠️ ❌ *The furnitures are new.* ❌ *She gave me an advice.* ❌ *many informations*

To count them, use a measure phrase: **a piece of** advice/furniture/information · **an item of** luggage · **a loaf of** bread · **a grain of** rice · **a bar of** soap · **a slice of** cake

### 1.5 Possessive Nouns

| Situation | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Singular noun | add **'s** | the boy**'s** bag |
| Plural ending in -s | add **'** only | the boys**'** bags |
| Irregular plural | add **'s** | the children**'s** toys, the men**'s** room |
| Name ending in -s | either | James**'s** book / James**'** book |
| Joint ownership | 's on the last name only | Ali and Siti**'s** house (one house, shared) |
| Separate ownership | 's on both | Ali**'s** and Siti**'s** houses (two houses) |

> ⚠️ **Never** use an apostrophe to make a plural. ❌ *Three apple's.* ✅ Three apples.

## 2. Pronouns

A pronoun replaces a noun.

### 2.1 The Table

| Person | Subject | Object | Possessive adjective | Possessive pronoun | Reflexive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sing. | I | me | my | mine | myself |
| 2nd | you | you | your | yours | yourself / yourselves |
| 3rd sing. m. | he | him | his | his | himself |
| 3rd sing. f. | she | her | her | hers | herself |
| 3rd sing. n. | it | it | its | its | itself |
| 1st pl. | we | us | our | ours | ourselves |
| 3rd pl. | they | them | their | theirs | themselves |

**Subject pronoun** = does the action. **Object pronoun** = receives the action.

> ⚠️ **The compound-subject trap** — the most common PSLE pronoun error.
> ❌ *Jia Wei and **me** went to the library.* → ✅ Jia Wei and **I** went to the library.
> ❌ *Mother gave the sweets to Ravi and **I**.* → ✅ Mother gave the sweets to Ravi and **me**.
> **Test:** remove the other person. "*Me* went to the library" is wrong → use **I**. "Mother gave the sweets to *I*" is wrong → use **me**.

> ⚠️ **its vs it's** — `its` = belonging to it. `it's` = it is / it has.
> The dog wagged **its** tail. · **It's** raining.

> ⚠️ **There is no word "theirselves" or "hisself".** Use *themselves* and *himself*.

### 2.2 Reflexive Pronouns

Used when the subject and object are the same person: *She hurt **herself**.*
Also for emphasis: *The principal **himself** presented the prize.*

> ⚠️ ❌ *He washed himself the car.* Reflexives are not used after every verb. Say: *He washed the car himself.*

### 2.3 Indefinite Pronouns — Singular

These look plural but are **grammatically singular**:

**everyone, everybody, everything, someone, somebody, something, anyone, anybody, anything, no one, nobody, nothing, each, either, neither, one**

> ✅ Everyone **has** finished **his or her** work.
> ✅ Neither of the boys **was** present.
> ⚠️ ❌ *Everybody are here.*

### 2.4 Relative Pronouns

**who** (people, subject) · **whom** (people, object) · **whose** (possession) · **which** (things/animals) · **that** (people or things, defining clauses only)

Covered fully in Section 15.

## 3. Articles and Determiners

### 3.1 A / An / The

| Article | Use |
|---|---|
| **a** | before a *consonant sound*: a book, a university (yoo-), a one-way street (wun-) |
| **an** | before a *vowel sound*: an apple, an hour (our-), an MRT station (em-), an X-ray |
| **the** | something specific, or already mentioned, or unique |

It is the **sound**, not the letter, that matters.
- **an** honest man, **an** heir, **an** hour (silent h)
- **a** European, **a** uniform, **a** union (yoo sound)
- **an** SMS, **an** FBI agent, **a** UN meeting

### 3.2 When to use "the"

- Something already mentioned: *I saw a dog. **The** dog was limping.*
- Unique things: **the** sun, **the** moon, **the** sky, **the** world
- Superlatives: **the** tallest boy, **the** first prize
- Rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, groups of islands: **the** Nile, **the** Pacific Ocean, **the** Himalayas, **the** Philippines
- Musical instruments: play **the** piano

### 3.3 When to use NO article (zero article)

- Plural or uncountable nouns in general: *__ Cats are independent.* *__ Water is essential.*
- Names of people, most countries, cities, streets, single mountains, lakes: Singapore, Mount Everest, Lake Toba
- Meals, sports, languages, school subjects: have __ lunch, play __ football, speak __ Malay
- Fixed phrases: go to **school/church/bed/hospital/prison** (when there for the purpose), by **bus/car/train**, at **home**

> ⚠️ *He went to **school**.* (as a student) vs *He went to **the** school.* (as a visitor, e.g. a parent)

### 3.4 Quantifiers

| Countable | Uncountable | Both |
|---|---|---|
| many, few, a few, fewer, several, a number of, each, every | much, little, a little, less, a great deal of, a large amount of | some, any, a lot of, lots of, plenty of, most, all, enough, no |

> ⚠️ **fewer vs less** — *fewer* for things you can count, *less* for things you cannot.
> ✅ **Fewer** cars, **less** traffic. ✅ **Fewer** students, **less** noise.

> ⚠️ **few vs a few** — *few* = almost none (negative). *a few* = some (positive).
> *He has **few** friends.* (he is lonely) · *He has **a few** friends.* (he has some)
> Same for **little** / **a little**.

## 4. Subject–Verb Agreement

The verb must match the subject in number. **This is the highest-frequency grammar question type in PSLE.**

### 4.1 Basic Rule

Singular subject → singular verb (usually ends in **-s**)
Plural subject → plural verb (no -s)

*The boy **runs**.* / *The boys **run**.*

### 4.2 The Rules That Actually Get Tested

**① Ignore words between the subject and verb.**
Cross out the interrupting phrase and check what is left.
- *The **box** of chocolates **is** on the table.* (box is → not chocolates are)
- *The **students** in the classroom **are** noisy.*
- Phrases to ignore: *along with, as well as, together with, including, in addition to, accompanied by, besides*
- *The **teacher**, as well as the students, **was** late.*

**② Two subjects joined by "and" → plural.**
- *Ali **and** Ben **are** friends.*
- **Exception:** if the two nouns are one single thing → singular. *Bread and butter **is** my breakfast.* *The **secretary and treasurer** **is** here.* (one person holding both posts)

**③ "Either…or" / "Neither…nor" / "Not only…but also" → the verb agrees with the NEARER subject.**
- *Neither the teacher **nor the students are** here.* (students → are)
- *Neither the students **nor the teacher is** here.* (teacher → is)

**④ Indefinite pronouns are singular.**
*Everyone, everybody, each, either, neither, someone, nobody, one* + **singular verb**
- *Each of the boys **has** a bag.*
- *Neither of the answers **is** correct.*

**⑤ Uncountable nouns are singular.**
- *The news **is** good.* *Mathematics **is** difficult.* *The furniture **was** delivered.*
- Also singular despite the -s: *news, mathematics, physics, economics, measles, mumps, athletics*

**⑥ Collective nouns** — singular if acting as one unit, plural if acting as individuals.
- *The team **is** winning.* (one unit)
- *The team **are** arguing among themselves.* (individuals)
- In PSLE, **singular** is safest unless the sentence clearly shows individuals.

**⑦ "A number of" vs "The number of"**
- *A number of students **are** absent.* (plural — means "many")
- *The number of students **is** increasing.* (singular — "the number" is one thing)

**⑧ Fractions, percentages, amounts** — depend on the noun that follows.
- *Half of the cake **is** gone.* / *Half of the students **are** here.*
- But amounts of money/time/distance as one unit → singular: *Twenty dollars **is** too much.* *Five kilometres **is** a long walk.*

**⑨ Plural-only nouns take plural verbs**, but with "a pair of" they become singular.
- *My trousers **are** torn.* / *A pair of trousers **is** on the bed.*

**⑩ "There is / There are"** — the verb agrees with what comes AFTER it.
- *There **is** a book on the desk.* / *There **are** some books on the desk.*
- *There **were** many people at the fair.*

**⑪ Titles and single names are singular.**
- *"The Three Little Pigs" **is** my favourite story.*

**⑫ Relative clauses** — the verb agrees with the word *who/which/that* refers to.
- *He is one of the boys **who play** football.* (the boys play)
- *He is the only one of the boys **who plays** football.* (only one plays)

## 5. Tenses

### 5.1 The 12 Tenses at a Glance

| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect Continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Past** | I walked | I was walking | I had walked | I had been walking |
| **Present** | I walk / he walks | I am walking | I have walked / he has walked | I have been walking |
| **Future** | I will walk | I will be walking | I will have walked | I will have been walking |

### 5.2 Simple Present

**Use:** habits, routines, facts, general truths, timetables.
**Signal words:** always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Mondays, generally, seldom, rarely

- *She **goes** to school by bus every day.*
- *Water **boils** at 100 °C.*
- Add **-s/-es** only for **he, she, it** (3rd person singular): *he play**s***, *she watch**es***, *it fli**es***

### 5.3 Present Continuous

**Form:** am/is/are + verb-ing
**Use:** happening now, temporary situations, planned future arrangements.
**Signal words:** now, at the moment, currently, right now, look!, listen!

- ***Listen!** The baby **is crying**.*
- *He **is staying** with his aunt this week.* (temporary)

> ⚠️ **Stative verbs are not used in continuous form:** *know, believe, understand, like, love, hate, want, need, prefer, own, belong, contain, seem, remember, forget, hear, see, smell (state)*
> ❌ *I **am knowing** the answer.* → ✅ I **know** the answer.

### 5.4 Simple Past

**Use:** a completed action at a definite past time.
**Signal words:** yesterday, last week/month/year, ago, in 2019, just now, once upon a time

- *We **visited** Malacca last December.*

**Spelling of regular past tense:**
- add **-ed**: walk → walked
- ends in -e: add **-d**: like → liked
- consonant + y → **-ied**: cry → cried, carry → carried
- one-syllable, CVC pattern → double the last consonant: stop → stopped, plan → planned, rob → robbed

### 5.6 Past Continuous

**Form:** was/were + verb-ing
**Use:** an action in progress in the past, often interrupted by a shorter action.

- *I **was watching** television **when** the phone **rang**.*
- Two actions at the same time: *While Mother **was cooking**, Father **was washing** the car.*

> **The classic pattern:** long action = past continuous, short interrupting action = simple past. *While I **was crossing** the road, a car **knocked** me down.*

### 5.7 Present Perfect

**Form:** have/has + past participle
**Use:**
- Action finished but the **time is not stated** or is unfinished: *I **have finished** my homework.*
- Experience: *She **has been** to Japan twice.*
- Started in the past, still continuing: *We **have lived** here **since** 2018.*

**Signal words:** already, just, yet, ever, never, since, for, so far, recently, lately, up to now

> ⚠️ **Never use present perfect with a finished past time expression.**
> ❌ *I **have seen** him yesterday.* → ✅ I **saw** him yesterday.

> **since vs for:** *since* + a point in time (since Monday, since 2019, since he left). *for* + a length of time (for three years, for two hours).

### 5.8 Past Perfect

**Form:** had + past participle
**Use:** the **earlier** of two past actions.

- *The train **had left** by the time we **reached** the station.*
- *He **had eaten** his lunch **before** he **went** out.*

> Only use past perfect when you need to show that one past event happened before another. If the order is already clear and simple, simple past is enough.

### 5.9 Future Forms

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **will** + verb | prediction, decision made now, promise | *I **will help** you.* |
| **going to** + verb | plan already decided, or evidence-based prediction | *We **are going to** visit Grandma.* / *Look at those clouds — it **is going to** rain.* |
| present continuous | fixed arrangement | *I **am meeting** her at 3 p.m.* |
| simple present | timetable | *The train **leaves** at 6 a.m.* |

> ⚠️ **After "when, before, after, until, as soon as, by the time" referring to the future, use the PRESENT tense, not "will".**
> ❌ *I will call you when I **will reach** home.* → ✅ *I will call you when I **reach** home.*

### 5.10 Tense Consistency

Within a narrative, stay in one tense. Most PSLE compositions are written in the **simple past**. Switch tense only when there is a reason (e.g. dialogue, a flashback in past perfect, a general truth).

## 6. Adjectives

An adjective describes a noun.

### 6.1 Order of Adjectives

When several adjectives stack up: **O-S-A-S-C-O-M-P**

**Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Colour → Origin → Material → Purpose**

*a beautiful (opinion) little (size) old (age) round (shape) brown (colour) Italian (origin) wooden (material) dining (purpose) table*

### 6.2 Comparative and Superlative

| Type | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| 1 syllable | add **-er**: tall → taller | add **-est**: tallest |
| 1 syllable, CVC | double consonant: big → bigger | biggest |
| 2 syllables ending in -y | y → **-ier**: happy → happier | happiest |
| 2+ syllables | **more** + adj: more careful | **most** careful |

**Irregular:** good → better → best · bad → worse → worst · little → less → least · many/much → more → most · far → farther/further → farthest/furthest

**Structures:**
- *A is **taller than** B.*
- *A is **the tallest** of the three.* (superlative needs **the**)
- *A is **as tall as** B.* (equal)
- *A is **not as tall as** B.*

> ⚠️ ❌ *more taller*, ❌ *most tallest* — never double up.
> ⚠️ Use the **comparative** for two things, the **superlative** for three or more.
> *She is the **taller** of the two sisters.* / *She is the **tallest** of the three sisters.*

### 6.3 -ed vs -ing Adjectives

**-ed** describes how a person **feels**. **-ing** describes what **causes** the feeling.

- *I was **bored** because the lesson was **boring**.*
- *The children were **excited** about the **exciting** trip.*
- Pairs: interested/interesting, tired/tiring, frightened/frightening, surprised/surprising, confused/confusing, amazed/amazing, disappointed/disappointing, annoyed/annoying, relaxed/relaxing

## 7. Adverbs

An adverb describes a verb, an adjective, or another adverb.

### 7.1 Types

| Type | Answers | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Manner | How? | quickly, carefully, well, hard, loudly |
| Time | When? | yesterday, soon, now, later, already, still |
| Place | Where? | here, there, everywhere, outside, nearby |
| Frequency | How often? | always, usually, often, sometimes, seldom, rarely, never |
| Degree | To what extent? | very, quite, extremely, rather, almost, too, enough |

### 7.2 Forming Adverbs

- Most: adjective + **-ly** → quick → quickly
- ends in -y: y → **-ily** → happy → happily, easy → easily
- ends in -le: drop e, add -y → gentle → gently, simple → simply
- ends in -ic: add **-ally** → dramatic → dramatically (exception: public → publicly)

**Same form as adjective:** fast, hard, late, early, straight, high, low, near, daily
**Irregular:** good → **well**

> ⚠️ **hard vs hardly** — *hard* = with effort. *hardly* = almost not.
> *He works **hard**.* (he is diligent) vs *He **hardly** works.* (he is lazy)
> Similarly: *late* (not on time) vs *lately* (recently); *near* (close) vs *nearly* (almost).

### 7.3 Position of Adverbs of Frequency

They go **before the main verb** but **after the verb "to be"**.
- *She **always** arrives early.*
- *She **is always** early.*
- With auxiliaries, they go after the first auxiliary: *He has **never** been late.*

### 7.4 Adjective or Adverb?

Adjectives describe nouns; adverbs describe verbs.
- ❌ *He ran **quick**.* → ✅ *He ran **quickly**.*
- ✅ *He is a **quick** runner.*

**But after linking verbs (be, seem, appear, look, feel, taste, smell, sound, become), use an ADJECTIVE:**
- *The soup tastes **good**.* (not *well*)
- *She looks **sad**.* (not *sadly*)
- *He feels **bad** about it.* (not *badly*)

## 8. Prepositions

### 8.1 Time

| Preposition | Use | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **at** | clock time, festivals, points | at 5 p.m., at noon, at night, at Christmas, at the weekend |
| **on** | days, dates, specific days | on Monday, on 5 May, on my birthday, on Sunday morning |
| **in** | months, years, seasons, parts of day, long periods | in May, in 2024, in summer, in the morning, in the 1990s |
| **for** | duration | for three hours |
| **since** | starting point | since Monday |
| **by** | not later than | Finish by 6 p.m. |
| **until/till** | up to a time | Wait until 6 p.m. |
| **during** | within a period | during the holidays |
| **from…to** | span | from Monday to Friday |

### 8.2 Place

| Preposition | Use |
|---|---|
| **at** | a point / specific address / event | at the bus stop, at 25 Jalan Kayu, at the concert |
| **on** | a surface / street / floor level | on the table, on the wall, on Orchard Road, on the third floor |
| **in** | an enclosed space / town / country | in the box, in Singapore, in the room |
| **between** | two things | between the two shops |
| **among** | three or more | among the crowd |
| **beside** | next to | beside the river |
| **besides** | in addition to | Besides Maths, I like Science. |
| **over / above** | higher than | The lamp hangs over the table. |
| **under / below** | lower than | The cat is under the chair. |
| **opposite** | facing | opposite the library |

### 8.3 Movement

**to** (destination) · **into** (entering) · **out of** · **onto** · **towards** · **through** (inside something) · **across** (from one side to another) · **along** · **past** · **up / down** · **off** · **from**

> ⚠️ We say *arrive **at*** a small place, *arrive **in*** a city/country, but *reach* takes **no preposition**: ✅ *I reached home.* ❌ *I reached to home.*
> ⚠️ *go home*, *reach home* — **no "to"**. ✅ *He went home.*

### 8.4 Dependent Prepositions (memorise as pairs)

| Verb + prep | Adjective + prep | Noun + prep |
|---|---|---|
| depend **on** | afraid **of** | reason **for** |
| listen **to** | good **at** | solution **to** |
| look **for** (search) | bad **at** | cause **of** |
| look **after** (care) | interested **in** | answer **to** |
| look **at** | proud **of** | increase **in** |
| wait **for** | tired **of** | effect **on** |
| apologise **to** (person) **for** (thing) | famous **for** | attitude **towards** |
| congratulate **on** | responsible **for** | need **for** |
| consist **of** | similar **to** | reply **to** |
| suffer **from** | different **from** | example **of** |
| believe **in** | keen **on** | invitation **to** |
| succeed **in** | full **of** | |
| result **in** (cause) / **from** (be caused by) | worried **about** | |
| accuse **of** | jealous **of** | |
| blame (someone) **for** | grateful **to** (person) **for** (thing) | |
| complain **about** | guilty **of** | |
| insist **on** | kind **to** | |
| prevent **from** | disappointed **with/in** | |
| remind **of** | satisfied **with** | |
| rely **on** | ashamed **of** | |
| agree **with** (person) / **to** (proposal) / **on** (matter) | angry **with** (person) / **about** (thing) | |

> ⚠️ **No preposition needed after:** discuss, enter, marry, resemble, lack, emphasise, approach, mention, request, answer (a question), attack, reach
> ❌ *discuss **about** the plan* → ✅ *discuss the plan*
> ❌ *enter **into** the room* → ✅ *enter the room*

## 9. Conjunctions and Connectors

### 9.1 Coordinating Conjunctions — FANBOYS

**For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So** — join two equal parts. Use a comma before them when joining two full sentences.

*It was raining**, so** we stayed indoors.*

### 9.2 Subordinating Conjunctions

They introduce a dependent clause.

| Meaning | Conjunctions |
|---|---|
| Time | when, while, before, after, until, since, as soon as, whenever, once, by the time |
| Reason | because, since, as, now that |
| Purpose | so that, in order that, lest |
| Result | so…that, such…that |
| Contrast | although, though, even though, while, whereas |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that, in case, as long as |
| Comparison | than, as…as |

> ⚠️ **Do not use two conjunctions for one relationship.**
> ❌ *Although he was tired, **but** he continued.* → ✅ *Although he was tired, he continued.* OR *He was tired, **but** he continued.*
> ❌ ***Because** it rained, **so** we stayed home.* → ✅ *Because it rained, we stayed home.*

### 9.3 Correlative Conjunctions (used in pairs)

both…and · either…or · neither…nor · not only…but also · whether…or · no sooner…than · hardly…when · so…that · such…that

> ⚠️ Keep the structures parallel: ✅ *She is **not only** clever **but also** hardworking.* (adjective + adjective)
> ❌ *She **not only** is clever **but also** she works hard.*

### 9.4 Linking Words / Connectors (crucial for composition and comprehension)

| Purpose | Connectors |
|---|---|
| Adding | furthermore, moreover, in addition, besides, also, what is more |
| Contrasting | however, nevertheless, on the other hand, in contrast, conversely, yet, still |
| Cause | because of, due to, owing to, as a result of, thanks to |
| Effect | therefore, thus, consequently, as a result, hence, accordingly |
| Sequence | firstly, next, then, meanwhile, subsequently, afterwards, finally, eventually |
| Example | for example, for instance, such as, namely, in particular |
| Emphasis | indeed, in fact, above all, certainly, undoubtedly |
| Summary | in conclusion, to sum up, overall, in short, all in all |

> ⚠️ **because vs because of** — *because* + a clause (subject + verb). *because of* + a noun.
> ✅ *He was late **because** the bus broke down.* ✅ *He was late **because of** the traffic jam.*
> Same for *due to / owing to* (+ noun) vs *since / as* (+ clause).

## 10. Modal Verbs

**can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must, ought to, need, dare**

| Modal | Main uses |
|---|---|
| **can** | ability (I can swim), informal permission, possibility |
| **could** | past ability, polite request, less certain possibility |
| **may** | formal permission, possibility |
| **might** | weaker possibility |
| **will** | future, willingness, promise |
| **would** | polite request, past habit, hypothetical |
| **shall** | suggestion/offer (*Shall we go?*), formal future with I/we |
| **should / ought to** | advice, expectation |
| **must** | strong obligation, logical certainty |
| **have to** | external obligation |
| **must not** | prohibition |
| **need not / don't have to** | no obligation |

**Rules:**
1. A modal is always followed by the **base form** of the verb. ❌ *She can **swims**.* ✅ *She can **swim**.*
2. Modals never take **-s** for he/she/it. ❌ *He cans.*
3. Two modals cannot be used together. ❌ *He will can come.* ✅ *He will **be able to** come.*

> ⚠️ **must not vs need not** — *You **must not** enter* = forbidden. *You **need not** enter* = you don't have to.
> ⚠️ **should have + past participle** = something that didn't happen but ought to have. *You **should have told** me.*

## 11. Sentence Structures and Clauses

### 11.1 The Four Types

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | one independent clause | *The boy ran home.* |
| **Compound** | two independent clauses joined by FANBOYS or ; | *The boy ran home, **and** he locked the door.* |
| **Complex** | one independent + one or more dependent clauses | ***Because he was frightened**, the boy ran home.* |
| **Compound-complex** | two independent + at least one dependent | ***When the bell rang**, the boy ran home **and** he locked the door.* |

Good compositions **vary** these. A paragraph of only simple sentences reads like P1 writing; a paragraph of only long sentences is exhausting. Mix them.

### 11.2 Clause Types

- **Independent (main) clause** — makes sense alone: *He smiled.*
- **Dependent (subordinate) clause** — cannot stand alone: *because he was happy*
  - **Adverbial clause:** tells when/why/how/where — *He smiled **because he was happy**.*
  - **Relative (adjective) clause:** describes a noun — *The boy **who won** smiled.*
  - **Noun clause:** acts as a noun — *I know **what he did**.*

### 11.3 Two Errors To Kill

**Sentence fragment** — an incomplete sentence punctuated as a full one.
❌ *Because it was raining heavily.* → ✅ *Because it was raining heavily, we cancelled the picnic.*

**Run-on / comma splice** — two full sentences joined by only a comma.
❌ *It was raining, we stayed indoors.*
✅ *It was raining**, so** we stayed indoors.* / *It was raining. We stayed indoors.* / *It was raining**;** we stayed indoors.*

### 11.4 Dangling Modifiers

The opening phrase must describe the subject that follows.
❌ *Walking to school, the rain started to fall.* (the rain was not walking)
✅ *Walking to school, **I** felt the rain start to fall.*

## 12. Punctuation

| Mark | Rules |
|---|---|
| **. Full stop** | End of a statement. Also in some abbreviations (Dr., a.m.) |
| **? Question mark** | End of a direct question only. Not for reported questions: *He asked where I lived.* |
| **! Exclamation mark** | Strong feeling or a command. Use sparingly in composition — one or two per essay. |
| **, Comma** | Separating items in a list · after an introductory phrase/clause · before FANBOYS joining two sentences · around extra information · before/after direct speech · after "Yes/No/Well" · direct address (*Come here, Ben.*) |
| **' Apostrophe** | Possession (*the boy's bag*) · contractions (*don't, it's, they're*) |
| **" " Inverted commas** | Direct speech · titles of stories/poems |
| **: Colon** | Introduces a list or explanation: *We need three things: bread, milk and eggs.* |
| **; Semicolon** | Joins two closely related full sentences: *It rained; we stayed home.* |
| **— Dash** | Adds a dramatic pause or afterthought |
| **( ) Brackets** | Extra, non-essential information |
| **Capital letters** | Start of sentence · proper nouns · "I" · days, months, festivals · titles · first word of direct speech |

### 12.1 Punctuating Direct Speech — Exact Rules

1. Speech goes inside inverted commas.
2. The first word of the speech is capitalised.
3. A comma (not a full stop) separates the speech from the reporting verb, and it goes **inside** the inverted commas.
4. A new speaker = a **new paragraph**.

```
"I am going home," said Mei Ling.
Mei Ling said, "I am going home."
"Are you coming?" she asked.
"Stop!" he shouted.
"If you finish early," Mother said, "you may play outside."
```

> ⚠️ Notice: when the speech is split, the second part starts with a **small letter** if it continues the same sentence.
> ⚠️ ❌ *"I am going home." said Mei Ling.* — full stop is wrong here, use a comma.

## 13. Active and Passive Voice

**Active:** the subject does the action. *The dog **bit** the boy.*
**Passive:** the subject receives the action. *The boy **was bitten** by the dog.*

### 13.1 How to Convert Active → Passive

1. Move the **object** to the front — it becomes the new subject.
2. Change the verb to **be + past participle**, keeping the original tense.
3. Put the old subject after **by** (or drop it if unimportant).

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple present | He **writes** a letter. | A letter **is written** by him. |
| Present continuous | He **is writing** a letter. | A letter **is being written** by him. |
| Simple past | He **wrote** a letter. | A letter **was written** by him. |
| Past continuous | He **was writing** a letter. | A letter **was being written** by him. |
| Present perfect | He **has written** a letter. | A letter **has been written** by him. |
| Past perfect | He **had written** a letter. | A letter **had been written** by him. |
| Future | He **will write** a letter. | A letter **will be written** by him. |
| Modal | He **can write** a letter. | A letter **can be written** by him. |

**Pronoun changes:** I → me, he → him, she → her, we → us, they → them
*She helped **me**.* → *I **was helped** by her.*

**Two objects:** either can become the subject; the more natural one is usually the person.
*He gave **me** **a book**.* → *I **was given** a book by him.* / *A book **was given** to me by him.*

**Drop "by…" when the doer is unknown or obvious:**
*Someone stole my bicycle.* → *My bicycle **was stolen**.*

> ⚠️ Verbs with no object (intransitive: *sleep, arrive, laugh, come*) have **no passive form**.

## 14. Direct and Reported Speech

**Direct:** *She said, "I am tired."*
**Reported:** *She said (that) she **was** tired.*

### 14.1 The Three Changes

**① Tense shifts back one step** (when the reporting verb is in the past):

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| simple present | simple past |
| present continuous | past continuous |
| simple past | past perfect |
| present perfect | past perfect |
| will | would |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| must | had to |
| shall | should |

**② Pronouns change** to match the new speaker's viewpoint.

**③ Time and place words change:**

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| last week | the week before / the previous week |
| next week | the following week |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |

### 14.2 Statements

*"I finished my homework yesterday," he said.*
→ *He said (that) he **had finished** his homework **the day before**.*

### 14.3 Questions

- Remove the question mark and the inverted commas.
- Change the word order to **statement order** (subject before verb).
- Use **asked / enquired / wanted to know**.
- **Wh-questions:** keep the question word.
  *"Where do you live?" she asked.* → *She asked where I **lived**.*
- **Yes/No questions:** add **if** or **whether**.
  *"Are you coming?" he asked.* → *He asked **whether/if** I **was** coming.*

> ⚠️ ❌ *She asked where **did I live**.* — do not keep question word order in reported speech.

### 14.4 Commands and Requests

Use **told / ordered / commanded / asked / requested / advised / reminded / warned + object + to + verb**.

*"Sit down," the teacher said.* → *The teacher **told us to sit** down.*
*"Please help me," she said.* → *She **asked me to help** her.*
**Negative:** *"Don't run," he said.* → *He **told me not to run**.*

### 14.5 Exclamations and Suggestions

*"What a lovely day!" she said.* → *She exclaimed that it **was** a lovely day.*
*"Let's go swimming," he said.* → *He **suggested going** swimming.* / *He suggested **that we should go** swimming.*

> **No tense shift** when reporting a general truth or something still true: *He said the earth **is** round.*

## 15. Relative Clauses

A relative clause gives more information about a noun.

| Pronoun | Used for |
|---|---|
| **who** | people (subject) — *The girl **who** won is my sister.* |
| **whom** | people (object) — *The man **whom** I met is a doctor.* |
| **whose** | possession — *The boy **whose** bag was stolen cried.* |
| **which** | things and animals — *The book **which** I read was thrilling.* |
| **that** | people or things, defining clauses only |
| **where** | places — *This is the house **where** I was born.* |
| **when** | times — *I remember the day **when** we met.* |

### 15.1 Defining vs Non-Defining

**Defining** — essential information, **no commas**, can use *that*.
*The students **who arrived late** were punished.* (only the late ones)

**Non-defining** — extra information, **commas required**, cannot use *that*.
*My brother, **who lives in Perth**, is a chef.* (I have only one brother)

> ⚠️ In non-defining clauses, use *who/which*, never *that*.
> ❌ *My father, that is a teacher, is strict.* → ✅ *My father, **who** is a teacher, is strict.*

### 15.2 Combining Two Sentences with a Relative Clause

This is a very common Synthesis question.
- *The boy is my cousin. He is wearing a red shirt.*
  → *The boy **who** is wearing a red shirt is my cousin.*
- *I lost the pen. My father gave it to me.*
  → *I lost the pen **which** my father gave me.*
- *That is the girl. Her mother is a nurse.*
  → *That is the girl **whose** mother is a nurse.*

> ⚠️ Do not repeat the pronoun. ❌ *The boy who **he** won…* ✅ *The boy who won…*

## 16. Question Tags

A short question added to the end of a statement.

**The rule: positive statement → negative tag; negative statement → positive tag.** Match the auxiliary verb and pronoun.

- *She is coming, **isn't she**?*
- *She isn't coming, **is she**?*
- *You finished it, **didn't you**?* (no auxiliary → use *do/does/did*)
- *They have left, **haven't they**?*
- *He can swim, **can't he**?*

**Special cases:**
| Statement | Tag |
|---|---|
| I am right, | **aren't I?** |
| Let's go, | **shall we?** |
| Open the door, | **will you? / won't you?** |
| Don't be late, | **will you?** |
| There is a problem, | **isn't there?** |
| Nobody came, | **did they?** (negative words: *nobody, nothing, never, hardly, seldom, few* make the sentence negative) |
| Everyone is here, | **aren't they?** |
| This is yours, | **isn't it?** |

## 17. Conditionals

| Type | If-clause | Main clause | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zero** | If + present | present | General truth: *If you heat ice, it **melts**.* |
| **First** | If + present | will + base | Likely future: *If it **rains**, I **will stay** home.* |
| **Second** | If + past | would + base | Unreal/imaginary present: *If I **were** rich, I **would** travel.* |
| **Third** | If + past perfect | would have + p.p. | Unreal past/regret: *If I **had studied**, I **would have passed**.* |

> ⚠️ **Never put "will" in the if-clause of a first conditional.** ❌ *If it will rain…* ✅ *If it rains…*
> ⚠️ In the second conditional, use **were** for all persons: *If I **were** you…*

**Unless** = if not. *You will fail **unless** you study.* = *You will fail **if** you **do not** study.*
> ⚠️ Never use *unless* with *not*. ❌ *Unless you don't study…*

## 18. Synthesis and Transformation

**Worth 10 marks in PSLE Paper 2 Booklet B (5 questions × 2 marks).** These follow fixed patterns. Learn the patterns and this becomes the most predictable section in the paper.

### The Golden Rules

1. **Keep the meaning exactly the same.** Do not add or remove information.
2. **Do not change the given words.** If the question gives you the start of the sentence, use it exactly.
3. **Check tense** — it must stay consistent with the original.
4. **Check for a full stop** and correct capitalisation.
5. **Re-read your answer once** — most lost marks are careless slips, not ignorance.

### Pattern Bank

**① although / though / even though ↔ but / however / despite / in spite of**
- *He was tired. He continued working.*
  → *Although he was tired, he continued working.*
  → *Despite/In spite of **being** tired, he continued working.* (despite + noun/gerund)
  → *Despite **his tiredness**, he continued working.*
> ⚠️ Never write *despite of*.

**② so…that ↔ such…that ↔ too…to ↔ enough…to**
- *The box was very heavy. He could not lift it.*
  → *The box was **so** heavy **that** he could not lift it.*
  → *It was **such a** heavy box **that** he could not lift it.*
  → *The box was **too** heavy for him **to** lift.*
  → *The box was **not light enough** for him **to** lift.*
> Structure: **so + adjective + that** / **such + a/an + adjective + noun + that**

**③ Passive voice** — see Section 13.
- *Someone has stolen my wallet.* → *My wallet **has been stolen**.*

**④ Reported speech** — see Section 14.
- *"I will call you tomorrow," she said.* → *She said (that) she **would call** me **the next day**.*

**⑤ Relative clauses** — see Section 15.

**⑥ not only…but also**
- *She sings. She dances too.* → *She **not only** sings **but also** dances.*
- Inverted (advanced): ***Not only does she sing, but she also dances.***

**⑦ Both…and / Neither…nor / Either…or**
- *Ali did not come. Ben did not come.* → ***Neither** Ali **nor** Ben came.*

**⑧ No sooner…than / Hardly…when / As soon as / The moment**
- *The bell rang. The pupils rushed out.*
  → ***No sooner had** the bell rung **than** the pupils rushed out.*
  → ***Hardly had** the bell rung **when** the pupils rushed out.*
  → ***As soon as** the bell rang, the pupils rushed out.*
> ⚠️ *No sooner* and *Hardly* at the start require **inversion** (had + subject + past participle), and *no sooner* pairs with **than**, *hardly* with **when**.

**⑨ unless / if not / provided that**
- *If you do not hurry, you will miss the bus.* → ***Unless** you hurry, you will miss the bus.*

**⑩ It was not until…that**
- *He only realised his mistake at midnight.* → ***It was not until** midnight **that** he realised his mistake.*

**⑪ By + gerund / Through**
- *He saved money. He walked to school.* → ***By walking** to school, he saved money.*

**⑫ Gerund / Infinitive as subject**
- *It is difficult to learn Chinese.* → ***Learning** Chinese is difficult.*

**⑬ so that / in order to / to (purpose)**
- *She woke up early. She wanted to catch the bus.*
  → *She woke up early **so that she could** catch the bus.*
  → *She woke up early **in order to** catch the bus.*

**⑭ because / because of / due to / owing to / as a result of**
- *The match was cancelled because it rained heavily.*
  → *The match was cancelled **because of/due to** the heavy rain.*

**⑮ Direct → Indirect questions / Embedded questions**
- *"Where is the station?" he asked.* → *He asked where the station **was**.*
- *Where is the station? Do you know?* → *Do you know where the station **is**?*

**⑯ prefer / would rather**
- *She likes tea more than coffee.* → *She **prefers** tea **to** coffee.*
- → *She **would rather** drink tea **than** coffee.*

**⑰ used to / be used to**
- *He played football regularly when he was young, but not now.* → *He **used to** play football.*
- *He is accustomed to waking early.* → *He **is used to waking** early.* (be used to + **gerund**)

**⑱ Comparatives**
- *No other boy in the class is as tall as John.* → *John is **the tallest** boy in the class.*
- *A is not as expensive as B.* → *B is **more expensive than** A.*

**⑲ Since / For (present perfect)**
- *He started living here in 2019.* → *He **has been living** here **since** 2019.*

**⑳ Too / Also / As well**
- *He was late. His sister was late.* → *He was late and **so was** his sister.*
- *He did not come. She did not come either.* → *He did not come and **neither did** she.*

### Synthesis Checklist (use on every question)

- [ ] Does my sentence mean **exactly** the same as the original?
- [ ] Have I used the **given word** correctly, in the right position?
- [ ] Is the **tense** right?
- [ ] Is the **subject–verb agreement** right?
- [ ] Did I **not add** any new information?
- [ ] Full stop and capital letter?
