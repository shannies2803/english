# English Workshop — how to open, publish and use it

*Audited 29 September 2026: 2,457 items, 4 papers, 0 structural errors. See `AUDIT_REPORT.md` for what was checked and what was fixed.*

The whole thing is **one file: `index.html`**. No server, no install. Double-click it and it opens in any browser, on a laptop, tablet or phone.

---

## Put it online free (pick one)

**Netlify Drop** — go to `app.netlify.com/drop`, drag the `site` folder in, you get a live URL immediately. Make a free account to rename it to something like `sengkang-english.netlify.app`.

**GitHub Pages** — create a free public repository, upload `index.html`, then Settings → Pages → Source: `main` / `/root`. Live at `https://yourname.github.io/reponame/`.

**Cloudflare Pages** — sign up at `pages.cloudflare.com`, choose Direct Upload, drop the folder in.

On a phone, once it's open: **Share → Add to Home Screen**. It then behaves like an app.

## The five tabs

**Today** — greeting, day streak, questions done, accuracy, and the suggested next set.

**Practice** — 99 sets, 2,218 questions (a further 239 sit inside the mock papers), grouped into Grammar & sentence work, Vocabulary, Comprehension, Visual text & listening, Writing, and Oral.
- Type-in questions are marked automatically. A one-letter spelling slip gets "So close — check the spelling" rather than a plain cross.
- Multiple-choice questions highlight the right and wrong options.
- Sentence-rewriting sets (punctuation, passive voice, reported speech, relative clauses, synthesis) show the model answer and ask the child to mark themselves honestly. There is no reliable way to auto-mark a rewritten sentence, and self-marking against a model is what they will do in real revision anyway.
- Cloze passages highlight which blank you are filling.
- Comprehension sets show the full passage above the question, with the mark allocation on every question, and a model answer to mark against.
- Every wrong answer is saved automatically.

**Mock papers** — four full papers: three complete Paper 2s (95 marks, 110 minutes each) and a Paper 1 Writing (55 marks, 70 minutes). Sit the Paper 2s several weeks apart — 2A first, 2C closest to the exam.

Paper 2 runs under exam conditions. A countdown timer runs, no feedback appears during the paper, and at the end the objective sections are marked automatically while written answers are self-marked one at a time against the model. You get a section-by-section mark sheet, an approximate AL band, and every objective question you missed is added to My mistakes.

**Listening** — the practice listening paper is in the Practice tab. An adult reads each text aloud twice from document 09; the script is also available in the app behind a clearly labelled adult-only toggle.

Paper 1 gives you the situational task and a themed composition with three pictures, a live word counter, and a full-height writing box. At the end you read your own work back and pick the band that honestly describes it, for Task fulfilment, Language and organisation, Content and Language — the same four scales the examiner uses. The mark sheet names your weakest scale so you know the one thing to work on.

**Listening** — two full 20-mark papers. Scripts for both are in document 09.

**Oral** — three conversation stimuli with model PEEL answers live in Practice. Four reading-aloud passages with pronunciation notes, plus home marking bands for both halves of Paper 4, are in document 10.

**Read & print** — the thirteen documents, with search, answer-masking and a clean print layout.

**My mistakes** — everything they got wrong, with the correct answer and the explanation. "Practise these" replays only those questions, and correct answers drop off the list. This is the Error Log from the study plan, running itself.

## Two learners

There are two profiles. Tap **Switch** to change learner, or to rename them. Each keeps its own progress, streak and mistake list.

The profile level changes what the app shows:
- **Primary 3–4** — 34 sets and 769 questions as the main list, with the P5–P6 material under "Coming later". Slightly larger text, violet accent.
- **Primary 5–6** — all 99 sets, teal accent.

The younger tier was deliberately built out in the latest round: plurals and nouns, pronouns, simple tenses, prepositions, adjectives and adverbs, punctuation, a 40-word spelling set of the words primary pupils most often misspell, everyday vocabulary, and two easier comprehension passages.

## Game mode

Switched on by default; the button at the bottom of **Today** turns it off per learner.

- **Experience and ranks.** Ten XP for a correct answer, four for a wrong one — effort still counts, so there is no reason to avoid a hard set. Twelve ranks from Rookie to Wordsmith Supreme.
- **Combo multiplier.** Consecutive correct answers build a combo: 1.25× at five, 1.5× at ten, 2× at twenty. One mistake resets it.
- **27 badges**, tied to real learning behaviour rather than time spent — retiring mistakes for good, clearing every due review in a day, scoring 100% on a set, finishing a mock paper, trying a set from every group.
- **Three daily quests**, which reset at midnight.
- **Boss Battle.** Fifteen questions drawn from the sets you score worst on and the mistakes you keep repeating. Twelve or more clears it. Unlocks after three sets, because before that there are no weak spots to draw from.
- **Head to head.** Both learners' experience this week, side by side. Resets on Monday.

Badges and rank-ups appear as brief notifications rather than pop-up dialogs, so they never interrupt a run.

## Where progress is stored

In the browser's own storage, on that device. Two things follow from this:

- Progress does **not** sync between devices. If the younger child practises on the iPad and the older one on the laptop, each device keeps its own record. That is usually what you want anyway.
- Clearing browser data, or using private/incognito mode, wipes it. If storage is unavailable, the app still works perfectly for that session — it just won't remember afterwards.

## What's new in this version

**Spaced repetition.** A wrong answer comes back after 1 day, then 3, then 7, then 21. Get it right four times running and it retires from the list. The Today screen shows how many are due; "Review what's due" plays only those. This is the single change most likely to raise a score, because it forces the same weakness back in front of the child until it stops being one.

**Starred questions.** Tap the star on any question, or press **S**. Practise them as a set from My mistakes.

**Beat the clock.** After finishing a set, replay it with 15 seconds a question. Useful for Booklet A, where speed is part of the test.

**Keyboard shortcuts.** `1`–`4` choose a multiple-choice option, `Enter` checks and then moves on, `S` stars.

**Search.** The box at the top of Practice searches set titles *and* the text of all 2,218 questions, so "passive" or "apostrophe" finds the right set.

**Weekly goal.** A bar on Today tracks questions answered this week against a target of 120 — roughly 25 a day, five days a week.

**Export and import.** Progress lives in one browser. The buttons at the bottom of Today write a backup file and read it back on another device, merging both learners' records rather than overwriting them.

**Printable worksheets.** From any finished set, "Print this set as a worksheet" produces a clean numbered sheet with answer lines, plus an answer key on a separate page.

**Situational writing practice.** Eight tasks with full model answers, a commentary on why each one scores, and the examiner's bands to mark yourself against.

**A large vocabulary and grammar expansion.** Grammar is now 1146 questions across 43 sets and Vocabulary 723 across 19. New this round: 150 more vocabulary MCQs graded across P3, P5 and P6; 40 synonyms and 40 antonyms; 50 word-form questions (decide → decision); 40 prefixes and suffixes; a second idioms set and a second phrasal-verb set; collective nouns and measure words; 50 more each of subject–verb agreement, tenses and prepositions; 40 more connectors; 40 pronouns and determiners; 40 modals and question tags; 30 each of passive voice, reported speech and relative clauses; three more editing passages; two more grammar cloze passages; and a new set on sentence fragments, run-ons and dangling modifiers.

**Vocabulary banks became practice.** Idioms, phrasal verbs, confusing word pairs and collocations were reference lists in document 04 with nothing to do with them. They are now four sets, 150 questions.

**Picture composition for Primary 3 and 4.** P3 and P4 sit a different composition paper from the older children: a four-picture series, all four pictures compulsory and in order, marked out of 20 rather than 40. Six tasks, each with the four panels drawn out, a full model composition and a note on what makes it work. Document 12 is the printable version, with the marking bands and a short note for parents.

**Reading aloud and composition planning moved into the app.** Eight passages to read out loud with self-marking bands, and ten six-minute planning drills where the model answer is a completed six-box plan rather than a finished story — because planning is the part most children skip.

## A suggested way to actually use it

Do not work through this front to back — it is a reference library, not a course.

1. Sit **Mock Paper 2A** cold in week one. It will feel too early. That is the point: you need a baseline.
2. Read the mark sheet. It names the weakest section.
3. Spend the next fortnight in that one group under **Practice**, and nowhere else.
4. Use **My mistakes** at the end of each week. Every wrong answer is already waiting there.
5. Re-test with **2B**, then **2C** closest to the exam.

One composition a week, timed at 50 minutes, marked against the rubric in document 08. Oral out loud twice a week from document 10 — it cannot be practised silently.

## Changing the content

The source is the twelve `.md` files plus the `content_*.json` files. Edit them, then run `parse_questions.py` (rebuilds the bank from the workbook and answer key), then `merge_content.py`, then `audit.py`, followed by `build_site2.py`. Both need Python and `pip install markdown`.
