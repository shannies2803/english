# Situational Writing — Twenty Tasks with Model Answers

Paper 1 Part 1 is worth **15 marks**: 9 for task fulfilment and 6 for language and organisation. Task fulfilment is worth more, which means covering every bullet point matters more than elegant writing.

Work through one task a week. Write your own answer first, cover the model, then compare.

---

## The marking bands

| Task fulfilment | | Language and organisation | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8–9 · All four points covered, each with a supporting detail | 9 | 6 · Correct format, consistent tone, almost no errors | 6 |
| 6–7 · All four covered, but one or two are thin | 7 | 5 · Correct format, a few errors, tone mostly right | 5 |
| 4–5 · Three points covered | 5 | 3–4 · Format mostly right, several errors or tone slips | 4 |
| 2–3 · Two points covered | 3 | 2 · Format or tone clearly wrong, frequent errors | 2 |
| 0–1 · One point, or largely off task | 1 | 0–1 · Hard to follow | 1 |

## Task 1 · Formal email · request

**The task**

Your class would like to hold a small farewell party for Mdm Lim, the school cleaner on your floor, who is retiring at the end of the month. You need the principal's permission.

Write an email to your principal, Mr Chandra. You should include:
• who the party is for and why
• when and where you would like to hold it
• what your class will do to keep the disruption small
• what you would like him to allow

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to request permission. Audience: the principal — formal. Context: four bullet points, each needing one supporting detail.

**Model answer**

```
To: chandra_r@riverbankpri.edu.sg
Subject: Request to hold a short farewell for Mdm Lim on 29 May

Dear Mr Chandra,

I am writing on behalf of Primary 6 Resilience. Mdm Lim, who has cleaned our floor for the past six years, will be retiring at the end of this month, and we would like to thank her properly before she leaves.

We hope to hold a short gathering in our own classroom on Friday 29 May, during the second half of recess, lasting about twenty minutes.

To keep the disruption to a minimum, we will use our own classroom rather than the hall, bring the refreshments ourselves, and clear everything away before the bell. Two pupils have volunteered to stay behind to check the room is spotless.

May we have your permission to go ahead, and would you be willing to let Mdm Lim know she is expected somewhere at that time without telling her why?

Thank you very much for considering our request.

Yours sincerely,
Rachel Tan
Primary 6 Resilience
```

**Why it scores:** All four points covered, each with one specific detail. Formal throughout — no contractions, no exclamation marks. The final request is concrete and even anticipates a practical problem.

## Task 2 · Formal email · complaint

**The task**

Your family ordered a birthday cake from Sweet Corner Bakery for collection on Saturday. When you collected it, the name written on it was wrong and the cake was a different flavour from the one ordered.

Write an email to the bakery manager. You should include:
• what you ordered and when
• what was wrong with the cake
• how this affected your family
• what you would like the bakery to do

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to complain and seek a remedy. Audience: a manager you do not know — formal. Stay polite; anger loses marks.

**Model answer**

```
To: manager@sweetcornerbakery.com.sg
Subject: Incorrect cake collected on Saturday 14 June (order no. 2187)

Dear Sir or Madam,

On Tuesday 10 June my mother ordered a chocolate cake for collection on Saturday 14 June, under order number 2187. The name "Arun" was to be written on it.

When we collected the cake, the name on it read "Aaron", and the cake was a strawberry sponge rather than the chocolate one we had chosen and paid for.

We only discovered this after we had returned home, by which time the guests had arrived, so we had to serve the cake as it was. My younger brother, whose birthday it was, was quite upset that his name had been spelt incorrectly in front of everyone.

I would be grateful if you could look into how the order was recorded, and I hope you will consider a refund or a replacement cake. I have kept the receipt and a photograph of the cake as it was given to us.

Thank you for your attention to this matter.

Yours faithfully,
Rachel Tan
```

**Why it scores:** Specific order details make the complaint credible. The effect on the family is described without exaggeration. The remedy requested is reasonable and the writer offers evidence rather than demanding.

## Task 3 · Informal email · invitation

**The task**

Your cousin Mei Hui lives in Penang. Your school is holding its first Open House and your class is running a stall. You would like her to come when she next visits.

Write an email to Mei Hui. You should include:
• what the Open House is and when it is
• what your class stall will be doing
• why you would like her to come
• one practical detail she needs to know

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to invite. Audience: a cousin your own age — informal. Contractions and questions are fine here.

**Model answer**

```
To: meihui.ong@email.com
Subject: Come to our Open House in September!

Hi Mei Hui,

How are things in Penang? I've got something to tell you about.

Our school is holding its very first Open House on Saturday 12 September, from ten in the morning until three in the afternoon. The whole school will be open and every class is running something.

Our class is doing a Science stall. We're building a marble run out of recycled cardboard, and visitors get to add one section to it, so by the end of the day it should stretch right across the classroom. I'm in charge of testing whether the marbles actually get to the bottom, which so far they mostly don't.

I'd really like you to come because you're the only person I know who'd enjoy this more than I do, and because I haven't seen you since Chinese New Year. You could stay over on the Friday night if your parents agree.

One thing to know: there's no parking at school that day, so your family would need to come by MRT and walk from Sengkang station. It's about eight minutes.

Let me know if you can make it!

Love,
Rachel
```

**Why it scores:** Warm and specific without gushing. The self-deprecating joke about the marbles sounds like a real child. The practical detail at the end is genuinely useful, not padding.

## Task 4 · Report

**The task**

You are the class environment representative. Write a report to your form teacher on your class's two-week effort to reduce paper waste.

You should include:
• what the class did
• the results
• one problem faced
• one recommendation

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to report factually. Audience: your form teacher. Use headings and a neutral tone — no chatty language.

**Model answer**

```
Report on the Primary 6 Resilience Paper Reduction Effort
By: Rachel Tan, Class Environment Representative
Date: 30 May 2026

Purpose
This report sets out what our class did during the two-week paper reduction effort from 18 to 29 May, and recommends one change for next term.

What we did
We placed a single scrap-paper tray beside the printer and agreed that no sheet would be thrown away until both sides had been used. Worksheets were printed double-sided. Three pupils volunteered to check the recycling bin each afternoon for paper that had been thrown away by mistake.

Results
Our class recycling bin was weighed each Friday. In the week before the effort began, it held 4.6 kilograms. In the second week of the effort, it held 1.9 kilograms, a reduction of just under sixty per cent.

Problem faced
The main difficulty was the scrap tray itself. Because it sat beside the printer, pupils frequently took clean paper from it by mistake, which caused some confusion and a few lost worksheets.

Recommendation
I recommend that the scrap tray be moved to the shelf by the door and labelled clearly on both sides. This would separate it from the clean paper and make it obvious what the tray is for.
```

**Why it scores:** Headings, factual tone, real numbers. The problem is admitted honestly and the recommendation follows directly from it — that link is where the marks are.

## Task 5 · Formal email · enquiry

**The task**

You have seen a notice about a free holiday coding workshop at the community centre but the notice is missing several details.

Write an email to the community centre. You should include:
• where you saw the notice and which workshop you mean
• two questions you need answered
• why you are interested
• how they can reach you

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to enquire. Audience: staff you do not know — formal. Questions must be clear and answerable.

**Model answer**

```
To: enquiries@sengkangcc.org.sg
Subject: Enquiry about the December Junior Coding Workshop

Dear Sir or Madam,

I saw a notice about the Junior Coding Workshop on the board outside your Level 2 counter last Saturday. The notice gave the dates as 1 to 5 December but did not state the timings.

I would be grateful if you could answer two questions. Firstly, what time does each session begin and end? Secondly, is the workshop suitable for someone who has never written any code before, or is some experience expected?

I am interested because I have been using a block-based coding app at school and would like to learn to write proper programs. My school does not offer this beyond Primary 6.

You can reply to this email address, or telephone my mother on 9123 4567 in the evenings.

Thank you very much for your help.

Yours faithfully,
Rachel Tan
```

**Why it scores:** Identifies the notice precisely, asks two genuinely distinct questions, gives a real reason for interest, and supplies two contact routes.

## Task 6 · Informal email · apology

**The task**

You promised your friend Daniel you would help him prepare for a chess tournament on Sunday, but you forgot and went out with your family instead. He played and lost badly.

Write an email to Daniel. You should include:
• what you did wrong
• why it happened, without making excuses
• how you think it affected him
• what you will do about it

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to apologise. Audience: a close friend — informal, but sincerity matters more than tone here. Avoid excuses.

**Model answer**

```
To: daniel.ong@email.com
Subject: I'm sorry about Sunday

Daniel,

I let you down on Sunday and I want to say so properly rather than just mumbling it at recess tomorrow.

I promised I'd come over on Saturday evening to go through openings with you before the tournament, and I forgot. My aunt arrived unexpectedly and we went out for dinner, and it simply went out of my head. I'm not offering that as an excuse — I could have sent you one message from the restaurant and I didn't.

I know you'd been preparing for a month, and I think going in without that last run-through probably shook your confidence before you'd even sat down. You told me twice that the openings were the part you were least sure of.

The next tournament is in August. If you still want my help, I'll come over every Saturday from July, and I'll put it in my mother's calendar so it can't happen again. If you'd rather ask somebody else, I'll understand.

I'm sorry.

Rachel
```

**Why it scores:** Owns the failure without softening it, and explicitly refuses to hide behind the excuse. Acknowledges the actual effect on Daniel. The remedy is concrete and leaves him the choice.

## Task 7 · Formal email · suggestion

**The task**

Your school canteen has long queues at the drinks stall every recess, and many pupils run out of time to eat.

Write an email to the head of the canteen committee. You should include:
• what the problem is
• evidence that it is a real problem
• one suggestion
• why your suggestion would work

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to suggest a change. Audience: a teacher in charge — formal. A suggestion is far stronger with evidence behind it.

**Model answer**

```
To: canteen_committee@riverbankpri.edu.sg
Subject: Suggestion to reduce the drinks stall queue at recess

Dear Mrs Devi,

I am writing about the queue at the drinks stall during the first recess.

Over the past two weeks, some classmates and I timed the queue on eight occasions. The shortest wait was six minutes and the longest was thirteen. Recess is twenty-five minutes, so a pupil who queues for drinks and then queues again for food often has under eight minutes left to eat.

I would like to suggest that bottled water and packet drinks be sold from a separate table at the far end of the canteen, staffed by a prefect, with exact change only.

This would help because roughly half the pupils in the queue are buying only a bottled drink, and they do not need the stallholder at all. Removing them from the main queue would shorten it considerably without requiring any extra staff or equipment.

I would be happy to help organise a trial for a week if that would be useful.

Thank you for considering this.

Yours sincerely,
Rachel Tan
Primary 6 Resilience
```

**Why it scores:** The evidence is the strongest part: actual timings, not an impression. The suggestion is cheap and specific, and the reasoning explains exactly why it would work.

## Task 8 · Informal message · declining

**The task**

Your friend Priya has invited you to her birthday outing on Saturday, but you have your grandmother's eightieth birthday dinner the same evening. You want to decline without hurting her feelings.

Write a message to Priya. You should include:
• thank her for the invitation
• explain clearly why you cannot come
• show you are genuinely sorry to miss it
• suggest something else

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to decline warmly. Audience: a close friend — informal and short. A declining message goes wrong when it is vague.

**Model answer**

```
Hi Priya,

Thank you so much for inviting me on Saturday — I was really pleased you thought of me.

I'm afraid I can't come. It's my grandmother's eightieth birthday that evening and the whole family is going for dinner. It's been booked since March and I'd never hear the end of it if I missed it, and honestly I wouldn't want to.

I'm genuinely sorry, because the trampoline park was the one thing I actually wanted to do this holiday, and I know you planned it around everyone's schedules.

Could I take you out for bubble tea the following Saturday instead, just the two of us? My treat, and you can tell me everything I missed.

Have a brilliant day.

Rachel
```

**Why it scores:** Thanks first, reason second, and the reason is specific enough to be believable. The regret is genuine rather than formulaic, and the alternative offered is concrete with a date.

## Task 9 · Formal email · appreciation

**The task**

Mr Devan, a veterinarian, gave a talk to your level about caring for pets. Your form teacher has asked you to write to him on behalf of the class.

Write an email to Mr Devan. You should include:
• what you are thanking him for
• one thing from the talk that your class found most useful
• what some pupils have done differently since the talk
• an invitation to a school event he might like to attend

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to thank, and to invite. Audience: an adult visitor you do not know well — formal. Context: four bullet points. The second and third are where most pupils go thin; each needs one specific example, not a general compliment.

**Model answer**

```
To: devan@greenlanevet.com.sg
Subject: Thank you for your talk to our Primary 5 pupils

Dear Mr Devan,

I am writing on behalf of Primary 5 Integrity to thank you for the talk you gave us last Wednesday on caring for pets. You gave up an entire afternoon of your working week for us, and we are very grateful.

The part the class found most useful was your explanation of how to tell when an animal is in pain. Many of us had assumed that a hamster or a rabbit would cry out if something were wrong. Learning that small animals hide their pain, and that a change in eating or grooming is often the only sign, was new to almost everybody in the room.

Since the talk, several pupils have begun keeping a short weekly note of what their pet eats. One of my classmates noticed that her rabbit had stopped finishing its hay, took it to a clinic, and found that it had a tooth problem that was caught early. She asked me to tell you that.

Our school is holding its Green Fair on Saturday 18 July from nine in the morning until noon, and there will be a stall on responsible pet ownership run by our class. We would be delighted if you could come, although we understand that weekends may be busy for you.

Thank you once again for your time and your patience with our many questions.

Yours sincerely,
Nathan Wong
Primary 5 Integrity
Green Lane Primary School
```

**Why it scores:** All four points covered, and each one carries a specific detail rather than a general compliment. The third point is the strongest move in the whole answer: instead of saying "we all take better care of our pets now", it tells one small true-sounding story with an outcome. Formal register is held from the greeting to the sign-off — no contractions, no exclamation marks — and the invitation is polite without being pushy.

## Task 10 · Informal email · instructions

**The task**

Your friend Haziq is coming to your flat on Saturday for the first time to work on a group project. He has never been to your estate.

Write an email to Haziq. You should include:
• how to get to your block from the MRT station
• what time to come and how long you expect the work to take
• what he should bring
• what to do if he cannot find the place

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to give directions and instructions. Audience: a friend — informal. Context: four bullet points. Directions must be in a sensible order, and the last point needs a real fallback, not "just call me".

**Model answer**

```
To: haziq.rahman@email.com
Subject: Getting to my place on Saturday

Hi Haziq,

Here's how to find my flat so you don't end up wandering around Yishun for half an hour.

Come out of Yishun MRT at Exit B, the one facing the bus interchange. Walk past the interchange and keep the big supermarket on your left. At the traffic light, cross over and you'll see three identical blocks — mine is the middle one, Block 726. The lift lobby is behind the bicycle racks, and I'm on the eleventh floor, unit 11-142. It's about eight minutes' walk in total.

Can you come at ten? I reckon we'll need about three hours for the model and the write-up, so we should be done by one. My mother says she'll feed us lunch, so don't eat before you come.

Bring your half of the research notes, a ruler and the glue gun if yours still works — mine gave up last week. I've already got the cardboard, the paint and the printer, so you don't need to worry about those.

If you get lost, call me on 9123 4567. If I don't pick up because I'm in the shower, wait at the mama shop next to the bicycle racks and I'll come down and find you — it's the only shop at the foot of my block, so you can't miss it.

See you Saturday!

Your friend,
Nathan
```

**Why it scores:** The directions run in the order the reader will walk them, with landmarks rather than street names, which is what actually helps. Informal tone throughout: contractions, a joke in the opening line, a question rather than an instruction about the time. The last point gives a genuine backup place to wait instead of the usual "just call me", which is what separates full task-fulfilment marks from partial ones.

## Task 11 · Report · to a teacher

**The task**

You are the class librarian. Your class ran a Reading Buddies scheme this term, in which Primary 6 pupils read with Primary 1 pupils every Friday. Your form teacher has asked for a report.

Write a report for your form teacher, Mrs Halim. You should include:
• how the scheme was run
• what went well
• one problem faced
• one recommendation for next term

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to report. Audience: your form teacher — formal and factual. Context: a report, so use headings, no greeting, no chatty language, and no "I think it was fun".

**Model answer**

```
Report on the Primary 6 Reading Buddies Scheme
By: Nathan Wong, Class Librarian, Primary 6 Integrity
Date: 14 August 2026

Purpose
This report describes how the Reading Buddies scheme ran in Term 3 and recommends one change for Term 4.

How the scheme was run
Twenty-four Primary 6 pupils were each paired with one Primary 1 pupil. The pairs met in the school library every Friday from 1.30 p.m. to 2.15 p.m. over eight weeks. Each Primary 6 pupil chose two picture books a week from a shelf set aside by the librarian, read one aloud to the younger child, and then listened while the child attempted the second. A record card was signed after every session.

What went well
Attendance was high: 21 of the 24 pairs met in at least seven of the eight weeks. The younger pupils grew noticeably more willing to read aloud; nine of them read a whole book unaided by Week 8, compared with two in Week 1. Several Primary 6 pupils reported that having to explain words made them think harder about meanings themselves.

Problem faced
The library was too noisy. Twenty-four pairs reading aloud in one room meant that many pupils could not hear their partner and began to read more loudly, which made the problem worse. Three pairs moved into the corridor without asking, which was unsafe.

Recommendation
The scheme should be split into two groups of twelve pairs meeting on alternate Fridays, or the second group should be given the use of an empty classroom. Twelve pairs in the library would allow each pair to hear each other without raising their voices.
```

**Why it scores:** Report format handled properly: a title, the writer's role, a date, and four headings — no "Dear Mrs Halim" and no sign-off. The language is factual and the evidence is numerical, which is what a report is for. The problem is a real operational one with a named consequence, and the recommendation follows directly from it rather than being a general wish for improvement.

## Task 12 · Notice · to schoolmates

**The task**

Your school is collecting used story books for children at a nearby welfare home. You have been asked to write a notice to be put up on the noticeboard at every level.

Write a notice. You should include:
• what is being collected and who it is for
• what condition the books must be in
• where and when to bring them
• who to approach with questions

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to inform and persuade. Audience: pupils across the whole school, including Primary 1 — semi-formal and very plain. Context: a notice, so use a heading, short lines, and no greeting or sign-off. A notice that a Primary 2 pupil cannot read has failed.

**Model answer**

```
BOOK DRIVE: GIVE A STORY A SECOND HOME

WHAT WE NEED
We are collecting used story books for the children at Sunrise Children's Home. They have a reading room with almost empty shelves. Picture books and easy chapter books are the most useful, because most of the children there are between five and ten years old.

WHAT WE CANNOT ACCEPT
Books must be clean, complete and readable. Please do not bring books with torn or missing pages, books that have been written in, or books that are damp. Textbooks and assessment books cannot be used.

WHERE AND WHEN
Bring your books to the two blue boxes outside the school library.
Collection runs from Monday 3 August to Friday 14 August, before school and during both recesses.
Please do not leave books on the floor if a box is full — tell the librarian instead.

QUESTIONS?
Speak to Nathan Wong of Primary 6 Integrity, or to Mrs Halim in the staff room.

One book from each of us fills a shelf. Thank you.
```

**Why it scores:** A notice has no greeting and no sign-off, and this one does not pretend to be a letter. Headings in capitals let a reader find one piece of information without reading the rest. The condition point is handled as a short "cannot accept" list, which is far clearer than a sentence, and the sentences stay short enough for a Primary 2 reader. The closing line persuades in seven words without slipping into an excitable tone.

## Task 13 · Formal email · explanation and appeal

**The task**

You borrowed a book from the public library and returned it three weeks late because it was left in your grandmother's flat in Malaysia during the holidays. You have been fined $6.30. You would like the library to reduce the fine.

Write an email to the library. You should include:
• the details of the book and the fine
• why the book was returned late
• what you have done about it
• what you are asking the library to do

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to explain and to appeal. Audience: a library officer you do not know — formal, and *Dear Sir or Madam* with *Yours faithfully*. Context: an appeal must accept responsibility first. A response that blames the library or sounds as though the fine is unfair loses language marks even when all four points are covered.

**Model answer**

```
To: enquiries@nlb.gov.sg
Subject: Appeal regarding an overdue fine of $6.30 — member 30098

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am a Primary 6 pupil and a member of the Punggol Regional Library. I returned a book called *The Boy at the Back of the Class* on 12 June, three weeks after it was due, and I have been charged an overdue fine of $6.30.

The book was late because I took it with me to my grandmother's home in Johor Bahru during the school holidays and left it in her flat by mistake. We did not travel back until the first weekend of June, which was when I recovered it. I understand that this was my own carelessness and not the library's fault.

I returned the book on the same day that it came back into my hands, and it is undamaged. I have since written the due dates of my loans on the calendar in my kitchen so that this does not happen again, and I have paid $2.00 of the fine from my own pocket money.

I would be grateful if the library would consider reducing the remainder of the fine, as $6.30 is a large amount for me to pay out of my allowance. If that is not possible, I would like to ask whether I may pay the balance in instalments over the next two months. I will accept whichever decision you reach.

Thank you for taking the time to read my appeal.

Yours faithfully,
Nathan Wong
Member number 30098
```

**Why it scores:** The first line identifies the writer and the matter in one sentence, which is what a formal opening is for. Responsibility is accepted explicitly — "my own carelessness and not the library's fault" — and this is the move that makes an appeal work. The fourth point offers an alternative in case the request is refused, which shows a genuine grasp of audience. *Dear Sir or Madam* is correctly paired with *Yours faithfully*, and the reference number appears in the subject line where an officer needs it.

## Task 14 · Informal email · breaking disappointing news

**The task**

You and your friend Priya were chosen for the school's badminton pair for the zonal finals. You have injured your wrist and the coach has replaced you. Priya does not know yet.

Write an email to Priya. You should include:
• what has happened
• how it happened and what the doctor said
• who will play with her instead, and what you think of that
• what you will do to support her

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to tell a friend bad news kindly. Audience: a close friend — informal, but not flippant. Context: the difficulty here is tone. The news is disappointing for the reader too, so an email that is only about your own feelings has missed the audience.

**Model answer**

```
To: priya.s@email.com
Subject: Some bad news about the finals — read this before training

Hi Priya,

I wanted you to hear this from me before you heard it in the hall. I can't play in the zonal finals with you. Coach Tan told me this morning that he has to replace me.

It's my right wrist. I landed badly on it during the last set on Tuesday and it swelled up overnight. My mother took me to the clinic on Wednesday and the doctor said it's a sprain, not a break, but I have to keep the splint on for three weeks and I'm not allowed to hold a racket at all. The finals are in nine days, so there was no way around it. I did ask.

Coach has put Wei Ling in with you. I know she plays from further back than I do and you'll have to cover more of the net at first, but she has a much better smash than mine and she doesn't panic. Honestly, I think the two of you could go further than we would have. It took me until yesterday evening to be able to write that sentence and mean it.

I'll be at every training session, and I'll be the loudest person in the gallery on the day. If it helps, I can film your games so you and Wei Ling can watch them back — I've got nothing else to do with my hands for three weeks. And I still owe you a rematch once this splint comes off.

Don't let this throw you. You were the better half of that pair anyway.

Your friend,
Nathan
```

**Why it scores:** The subject line tells the reader why to open the message now, and the first sentence delivers the news without a paragraph of build-up. The third point does the hardest thing in the task: it says something generous about the replacement and admits how hard that was to write, which is both honest and kind. The support offered is concrete — filming the games — rather than "I'll support you". The tone stays informal through contractions and short sentences without becoming jokey about news the reader will find upsetting.

## Task 15 · Informal note · to a classmate

**The task**

Your classmate Hui Ling was absent yesterday. Write her a short note.

You should include:
• what she missed in class
• the homework that was given
• an offer to help

Write about 60 to 80 words.

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to tell her what happened. Audience: a classmate — informal. Context: three points, each needing one detail.

**Model answer**

```
Hi Hui Ling,

I hope you are feeling better. You missed quite a lot yesterday.

In Science we started the new topic on magnets, and Mr Lee did an experiment with iron filings on a sheet of paper. It was the best lesson we have had this term.

For homework we have to finish Workbook pages 24 to 26, and write five sentences about what we saw in the experiment. It is due on Friday.

If you like, I can come over on Wednesday after school and show you my notes, because the experiment is hard to explain in writing.

Get well soon!

Your friend,
Dinesh
```

**Why it scores:** All three points are covered and each one carries a detail: not just 'we did Science' but which topic and which experiment; not just 'there is homework' but the pages and the due date; not just 'I can help' but when, and why helping in person would be better. The tone is friendly throughout and the note opens and closes properly.

## Task 16 · Informal email · thanking

**The task**

Your aunt sent you a birthday present. Write her an email.

You should include:
• thanks for the present
• what you have done with it so far
• news about something happening at school

Write about 60 to 80 words.

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to thank and to share news. Audience: an aunt you know well — warm and informal. Context: three points.

**Model answer**

```
To: aunty_meena@email.com
Subject: Thank you for the paint set!

Dear Aunty Meena,

Thank you so much for the watercolour set you sent for my birthday. It arrived on Saturday morning, and I was very surprised because I had not told anybody that I wanted one.

Love,
Sarah
```

**Why it scores:** The thanks are specific rather than general, and the second point does the most work: two real paintings, one of which went wrong, which is far more interesting than 'I have used it a lot'. The school news is a genuine piece of news with a detail attached. The closing line ties back to the joke, which is a nice touch at this level but not required.

## Task 17 · Short message · apologising

**The task**

You borrowed your cousin's book and spilt a drink on it. Write him a message.

You should include:
• what happened
• an apology
• what you will do about it

Write about 60 to 80 words.

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to apologise. Audience: a cousin — informal, but the tone must be genuinely sorry, not jokey.

**Model answer**

```
Hi Marcus,

I have some bad news about your book. I was reading it at the kitchen table yesterday evening and I knocked over a glass of Milo. The first twenty pages are stained brown and two of them are stuck together at the corner.

I am really sorry. It was completely my fault — I should not have been drinking anything next to it.

I have looked the book up online and it costs $14.90. I have $9 saved and I will have the rest by the end of the month, so I would like to buy you a new copy. If you would rather have it sooner, I can ask my mother and pay her back.

Sorry again. I will not borrow anything of yours near a drink again.

Dinesh
```

**Why it scores:** The apology works because the damage is described honestly and in detail before the sorry, rather than after it. Blame is accepted without excuses. The third point is concrete — a price, an amount saved, a date, and an alternative — which is what turns an apology into a solution.

## Task 18 · Informal email · inviting

**The task**

Your school is holding a Sports Day. Write an email inviting your grandfather.

You should include:
• when and where it is
• what you will be taking part in
• how he can get there

Write about 60 to 80 words.

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to invite. Audience: your grandfather — warm and clear. Context: he needs practical information, so the third point matters as much as the first two.

**Model answer**

```
To: ah_gong@email.com
Subject: Come to my Sports Day!

Dear Ah Gong,

Our school Sports Day is on Saturday 12 July, from 8 a.m. to 12 noon, on the school field.

I am running in the 100 metres at about 9.15, and I am in the class relay, which is the last event.

You can take bus 156 from your block and get off at the fourth stop, right outside our gate. If you would rather not, my father can fetch you at half past seven.

Please come!

Love,
Sarah
```

**Why it scores:** Every one of the three points carries a real detail, and the third — bus number, which stop, how long, plus an alternative — is the one most pupils leave thin. The joke about Amirul keeps the tone warm without losing the information. Notice that the writer tells him where she will be looking for him, which shows she has thought about the reader.

## Task 19 · Notice · to the class

**The task**

You are the class monitor. The classroom cupboard is untidy. Write a notice for the class noticeboard.

You should include:
• what the problem is
• what everybody should do
• when it must be done by

Write about 60 to 80 words.

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to tell everybody what to do. Audience: your classmates — plain and polite, not bossy. Context: a notice, so no greeting and no sign-off, but put your name at the end.

**Model answer**

```
PLEASE HELP: OUR CLASS CUPBOARD

THE PROBLEM
The cupboard at the back of the classroom is so full that the door no longer shuts properly. Two files fell out on Monday and the pages got mixed up. There are also three lunchboxes in there that have been left for more than a week.

WHAT TO DO
Please take out anything of yours that does not belong in school and bring it home. Put files on the top shelf, art materials on the middle shelf, and PE things on the bottom shelf. If something is not yours, leave it on my desk and I will try to find the owner.

BY WHEN
Please do this before recess on Friday. Mrs Ong will be checking the cupboard that afternoon.

Thank you!
Sarah (class monitor)
```

**Why it scores:** A notice has no 'Dear' and no 'Yours sincerely', and this one does not pretend to be a letter. The three headings let a classmate find what they need without reading it all. The instructions are specific enough to follow — which shelf for what — and the deadline has a reason attached, which makes it far more likely to be obeyed than a bare date.

## Task 20 · Informal email · describing

**The task**

You went on a school excursion last week. Write to your friend who has moved away.

You should include:
• where you went
• the best part of the day
• one thing that went wrong

Write about 60 to 80 words.

> **Before you write:** Purpose: to describe. Audience: a friend — informal and chatty. Context: three points; the third is the one that makes the email worth reading.

**Model answer**

```
To: jia_wei@email.com
Subject: Our trip to the wetland reserve

Hi Jia Wei,

You missed a good one. Last Thursday our level went to the wetland reserve at Sungei Buloh.

The best part was the mudskippers. One hopped onto a root a metre from the boardwalk and sat staring at us.

The thing that went wrong was our worksheet. Faizal put it on the railing for one second and the wind took it into the water, so we redid it from memory on the bus.

How is your new school?

Your friend,
Dinesh
```

**Why it scores:** The three points are all covered with detail, and the writer knows that the disaster is the most entertaining part, so it gets the fullest paragraph. Small precise facts — eleven minutes of rain, eight centimetres, station three — do more than adjectives. The two questions at the end keep it a real conversation rather than a report.

## The five most common ways marks are lost

1. **A bullet point missed entirely.** Tick them off on the question paper as you write.
2. **A bullet point mentioned but not developed.** Each point needs one supporting detail, not a bare sentence.
3. **Tone drifting.** "Dear Sir or Madam" followed by "Hey, so anyway" loses language marks immediately.
4. **A vague request.** "Please do something about it" earns less than "Please repair the zip or exchange the bag".
5. **Inventing facts that contradict the question.** If the question says two weeks ago, do not write last month.
