## 1. What the PSLE English Exam Actually Tests

Everything in this pack maps to one of these four papers. Nothing else is needed.

| Paper | What it is | Time | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paper 1** | Writing — Part 1 Situational Writing, Part 2 Continuous Writing (composition) | 1 h 10 min | 55 |
| **Paper 2** | Language Use & Comprehension | 1 h 50 min | 95 |
| **Paper 3** | Listening Comprehension | ~35 min | 20 |
| **Paper 4** | Oral — Reading Aloud + Stimulus-based Conversation | ~10 min | 30 |
| | | **Total** | **200** |

### Paper 2 broken down (this is where grammar lives)

**Booklet A (28 marks, MCQ)**
- Grammar MCQ — 10 marks
- Vocabulary MCQ — 5 marks
- Vocabulary Cloze — 5 marks
- Visual Text Comprehension — 8 marks

**Booklet B (67 marks, open-ended)**
- Grammar Cloze — 10 marks
- Editing (spelling & grammar) — 12 marks
- Comprehension Cloze — 15 marks
- Synthesis & Transformation — 10 marks
- Comprehension Open-Ended — 20 marks

> **Key insight for parents:** Grammar is worth roughly **32 marks** directly (MCQ + cloze + editing + synthesis), and it silently controls another ~20 marks of composition language. It is the single highest-return thing to drill. That is why this pack front-loads it.

## 3. Level Guide — What to Do When

You said P3–P6, so here is how to stage it. Grammar rules are cumulative: P6 content assumes P3 content is solid.

**Primary 3**
- Reference: Sections 1–8 (nouns, pronouns, articles, subject–verb agreement, simple tenses, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions)
- Practice: Sections A–F
- Writing: short 3-paragraph stories, focus on full sentences and correct tense
- Vocab: Thematic lists + "said/walked/looked" synonym banks

**Primary 4**
- Add: Sections 9–12 (conjunctions, modals, perfect tenses, punctuation)
- Practice: Sections G–J, plus first editing passage
- Writing: 5-paragraph structure, dialogue with correct punctuation
- Vocab: Idioms Set 1, confusing pairs

**Primary 5**
- Add: Sections 13–17 (passive voice, reported speech, relative clauses, question tags, conditionals)
- Practice: Sections K–P
- Writing: full picture-based composition under timing, situational writing formats
- Vocab: Idioms Sets 2–3, collocations, phrasal verbs

**Primary 6**
- Everything. Heavy focus on Section 18 (Synthesis & Transformation) — it is 10 marks and highly patterned, so it is the easiest place to gain marks fast.
- Practice: Sections N–R weekly, timed
- Writing: 1 composition per week, timed at 50 minutes, self-edited against the checklist

## 4. A Weekly Routine That Works Without Tuition

Roughly **4 hours a week**, split into short sessions. Short and daily beats long and weekly.

| Day | 30–45 min |
|---|---|
| **Mon** | Grammar: read one reference section, do the matching practice section |
| **Tue** | Vocabulary: learn 10 words / 3 idioms. Write one sentence for each. |
| **Wed** | Comprehension: one passage (open-ended). Or comprehension cloze. |
| **Thu** | Synthesis & Transformation: 10 questions, timed 10 min (P5–P6) |
| **Fri** | Editing + grammar cloze passage, timed |
| **Sat** | **Composition** — 50 min timed, then 15 min self-editing |
| **Sun** | Rest, or read a book for 30 min. Reading is not optional; it is the input that makes everything else possible. |

### The self-marking loop (this is the actual "tuition" part)

Tuition mostly works by giving feedback. You can replicate it:

1. Child does the work **without** the answer key nearby.
2. Child marks it themselves with a **different coloured pen**.
3. For **every wrong answer**, child writes in an **Error Log** notebook:
   - The question
   - What they wrote
   - The correct answer
   - **Why** they were wrong (one sentence, in their own words)
4. Re-attempt those same questions **one week later**.

The Error Log is worth more than any worksheet. Most children lose the same 6–8 marks repeatedly. Finding out *which* 8 is the entire game.

## 5. Reading — The Non-Negotiable

Vocabulary lists help, but children who read widely score higher on comprehension and composition because they absorb sentence rhythm and word usage naturally.

- **20–30 minutes daily**, any genre they enjoy
- Books slightly above their comfort level, not below
- Keep a small notebook: write down 3 new words per book, with the sentence they appeared in
- Newspapers (*The Straits Times*, *Little Red Dot* for primary) help enormously with Paper 4 Oral, since the conversation topics are usually current, everyday issues
